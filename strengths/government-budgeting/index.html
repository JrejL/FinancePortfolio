What the Numbers Say

  1. With no corrective action the reserve is exhausted and the fund balance turns negative in FY2028, at (4,746). The gap (net change in fund balance) widens from (6,408) in FY2026 to (10,237) in FY2030, a 3,830 deterioration: both departments and pension outgrow revenue, with departments the larger dollar driver (13,466 of added spending at 3.3% a year across six departments) against pension's smaller but faster growing 1,518 (nondepartmental, 8% a year).

What the Numbers Say

  1. The five options on the table close 4,630 of the FY2026 gap of (6,408), leaving (1,778) remaining; the transient occupancy tax increase needs voter approval.
  2. The General Fund posted three straight years of deficits: net change in fund balance was (1,450) in FY2023, (2,450) in FY2024 and (5,430) in FY2025, and the reserve ratio fell to 16.0%, below the 17% policy minimum.

A city's General Fund reports on modified accrual, so there is no cash flow statement; the budget comparison is the statement a council reads first.
